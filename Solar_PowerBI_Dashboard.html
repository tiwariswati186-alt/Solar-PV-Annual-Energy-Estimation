Top 4 Features Explained
What each driver means for solar output
Explainability
#1 — PV Size (kWdc) · 54.5%
System capacity is the dominant driver. A 50–100 kW system produces 17× more than a <5 kW system. Optimising capacity delivers the highest ROI.
#2 — DC/AC Ratio · 18.6% (Engineered)
Inverter efficiency indicator. Optimal ratio: 1.2–1.3. Systems below 1.1 underutilise the inverter. Above 1.4 causes energy clipping losses.
#3 — PV Size (kWac) · 12.2%
AC inverter output capacity. Complements kWdc — together they account for 66.7% of all predictive power in the model.
#4 — Division · 4.5%
Geographic region determines solar irradiance. Upstate NY (Geneva, Binghamton) averages 174K–193K kWh vs 6.4K median for NYC divisions.

Key Insights & Recommendations

Evidence-backed findings from 218,115 records and the trained ML model
📊 6 Key Findings from the Data
54.5%
of predictions driven by system size
PV capacity (kWdc) is the #1 driver. A 50–100 kW system produces 17× more than a <5 kW system. Size optimisation is the highest-ROI lever.
1.2–1.3
optimal DC/AC ratio
Our engineered DC/AC ratio feature (18.6% importance) reveals that systems in the 1.2–1.3 range produce the most energy per unit of capacity.
17×
output difference: large vs small
100kW+ systems produce a median of 352,011 kWh/yr vs 4,779 kWh for <5 kW systems. Scale delivers exponential, not just linear, gains.
18,560
new projects in 2023 (record high)
After a 2017–2020 dip, solar adoptions surged to a record in 2023 — 110% higher than 2020. The market is strongly recovering.
98.6%
installations use Net Metering
NM dominates across the dataset, which simplifies modelling. Future regulatory changes (CDG expansion, RNM) could shift this significantly.
4.5%
prediction power from location
Division + County together contribute 6.6% to predictions. Upstate NY regions average 174K–193K kWh vs 6.4K for dense NYC divisions.
